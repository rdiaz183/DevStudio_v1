Your TCG concept blends high-pacing mechanics with clear anti-snowball protections, but it contains a few design tensions to refine before playtesting.
Core Mechanics Breakdown & Rating
Commander & Ranking System (8/10): The Rank 0 to 3/4 progression directly addresses power creep and turn-1 blowout combos by forcing a cost/rank paywall. Making the Commander targetable only after clearing the 5-monster front line creates clear tactical depth. Tension: Ending the game immediately upon Commander destruction creates a sudden-death threat that conflicts directly with your prize system.
Prize Pool System (8.5/10): Taking damage to draw from a 6â€“8 prize card pool mirrors modern comeback mechanics. It rewards taking damage with hand option elasticity to prevent snowballing.
Energy & Resource System (9/10): The fixed +1 energy gain per turn (starting at 2) managed via a single die eliminates mana screw (MTG) and turn-1 over-extensions (Yu-Gi-Oh!). Providing Player 2 an extra energy on turn 1 cleanly balances the first-turn advantage.
Deck Limits & Composition (7.5/10): A 50-card main deck with a 4-copy limit balances consistency and resource conservation. Color-locking cards via the Commander keeps deck-building archetypes distinct.
Interaction & Speed (7/10): Sorcery vs. Item timing and LIFO chain priority offer solid back-and-forth. Tension: The tiebreaker rule (defender dies, attacker survives on equal ATK) heavily favors aggressive strategies, making defensive walls difficult to maintain.

Is There a Game Set Up Exactly the Same?
No single game has this identical combination, but your design is a direct hybrid of Cardfight!! Vanguard and One Piece Card Game:
Vanguard: Identical Grade/Rank progression (0 to 3), mill mechanic at start, and central commander concept.
One Piece: Identical Leader color-locking rules, 5-monster board limit, and ramping energy system without main-deck resource cards.
Digimon: Identical prize-card damage engine.
Critical Design Adjustments Needed for Playtesting
Win Condition Conflict: If taking damage draws a card from the 6â€“8 prize pool, but attacking the Commander directly kills it, clarify when direct attacks are allowed. If direct attacks can happen while prize cards remain, the prize pool becomes redundant. Consider having Commander attacks reduce the prize pool count instead.
Tiebreaker Combat: Standardizing attacker survival on equal ATK heavily nerfs defensive play. Testing mutual destruction (both cards die on equal ATK) will preserve board balance.
